# Story Patterns

Choose the closest pattern before writing pages.

## Habit / routine

Use for:
- washing hands/paws;
- brushing teeth;
- putting toys away;
- dressing;
- bedtime routine;
- sitting for meals.

Pattern:
1. Familiar activity begins.
2. A transition cue appears.
3. Desired action starts.
4. Action is shown clearly.
5. Action completes.
6. Positive practical result.
7. Calm closure if needed.

For toddlers, do not create a villain/problem just to teach the habit.

## Independence / trying

Use for:
- putting on clothing;
- using a spoon;
- carrying something;
- simple self-care.

Pattern:
1. Character wants to try.
2. Small attempt.
3. Difficulty is concrete and mild.
4. Help or another attempt.
5. Success or partial success.
6. Warm recognition of effort.

Avoid perfectionism.

## Emotion naming/regulation

Pattern:
1. Clear triggering situation.
2. Visible feeling.
3. Simple name for the feeling.
4. Safe supportive response/action.
5. Body/action settles.
6. Reconnection/closure.

Never present the emotion itself as bad.

## Fear / new situation

Use for:
- doctor/dentist;
- new place;
- loud sound;
- haircut;
- separation.

Pattern:
1. Introduce the situation truthfully.
2. Character feels unsure.
3. Show what will happen in concrete steps.
4. Give a predictable coping/support action.
5. Situation occurs without unnecessary dramatization.
6. Safe completion.

Do not promise that a real medical procedure will never hurt.

## Safety rule story

Use for:
- road;
- hot surfaces;
- stairs;
- water;
- unsafe objects.

Pattern:
1. Normal context.
2. Clear hazard cue.
3. Simple rule/action.
4. Adult support where appropriate.
5. Safe result.
6. Repeat rule in action, not as a lecture.

Do not depict dangerous behavior in a way that looks fun or instructional.

## Social skill

Use for:
- sharing;
- turn-taking;
- gentle touch;
- asking;
- waiting.

Pattern:
1. Two characters want/need something.
2. Tension is mild and recognizable.
3. Model a simple phrase/action.
4. Other character responds.
5. Both continue positively.
6. Close without shaming.

## Curiosity / explanation

Use for:
- why rain falls;
- where shadows come from;
- simple nature/science.

Pattern:
1. Child notices something.
2. Concrete question.
3. Exploration/observation.
4. Simple explanation through the scene.
5. One reinforcing example.
6. Satisfying final observation.

Accuracy is more important than fantasy when the book's purpose is explanatory.

## Family memory / documentary

Use for real events.

Pattern:
1. Begin in the real place/time.
2. Show concrete sensory/family detail.
3. Continue actual sequence.
4. Highlight connection or memorable gesture.
5. Show another true angle/detail.
6. End on a specific remembered image.

Do not invent conflict, achievement, diagnosis, or moral unless the parent explicitly requests fictionalization.
